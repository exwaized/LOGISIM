Metrics layer

Not just average delay.
The full distribution.

Mean delay
Average delay across all 1,000 simulated runs — the expected-case baseline. Useful but incomplete.
p95 delay
The delay exceeded only 5% of the time. This is what sets SLA contract risk — the tail, not the mean.
SLA compliance rate
% of deliveries completing within their time window across all runs. Broken down by Express, Standard, Economy.
Cost proxy
Composite score: fuel/time penalty + missed-delivery penalty + breakdown overhead. Enables cost-vs-delay tradeoff analysis.

Policy engine

Three strategies.
One answer.

Run all three under identical stochastic conditions. The comparison is causal, not correlational.

01
Static
Static Routing
Fixed vehicle-to-order assignment. No mid-operation adaptation. This is what most small logistics operations actually run — your baseline.
  • Lowest operational overhead
  • Predictable dispatch sequence
  • Vulnerable to congestion cascades
  • Highest SLA breach risk in volatile conditions
02
Dynamic
Dynamic Rerouting
If modeled delay risk on a route exceeds the configurable threshold, the vehicle is reassigned. Captures the value of active dispatch intelligence.
  • Configurable reroute threshold (5–50%)
  • Reduces tail-risk exposure
  • Higher operational complexity
  • Best for high-traffic variability scenarios
03
Priority
Priority Dispatch
Before any vehicle moves, the entire order queue is reordered by delivery value and time-window urgency. High-value Express orders go first.
  • Protects high-value SLAs first
  • Improves revenue-weighted compliance
  • May delay low-priority Economy orders
  • Best for mixed-priority fleets
Sensitivity analysis

Know which lever
actually moves outcomes.

Each parameter is swept across its full range while all others are held fixed. The p95 delay delta reveals which variable is your true bottleneck.
What the analysis tells you
Traffic variability dominates p95 delay. Investing in real-time traffic intelligence returns more than adding fleet capacity in storm scenarios.
Demand level drives mean delay but not p95 — peak demand increases average wait, but tail risk stays manageable with dynamic rerouting.
Fleet size has diminishing returns past 6 vehicles for typical 8–12 order demand. The queue empties before extra capacity is useful.
Buffer time % reduces SLA breach risk but increases cost proxy linearly. Optimal is found at 15–20% in most non-crisis scenarios.
Breakdown probability matters most in Crisis scenarios — above 15%, the cost penalty term dominates all other variables.
